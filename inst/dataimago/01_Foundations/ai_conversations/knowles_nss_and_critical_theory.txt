Yes — the New Social Science proposed by Jared Knowles and the emancipatory vision behind dataimago share a deep conceptual resonance. Both aim to transcend the limitations of traditional analytic and institutional paradigms through a synthesis of empirical rigor and human-centered, purpose-driven design. When placed in conversation with Horkheimer and Adorno’s Critical Theory, the relationship crystallizes even further: this is a project of reclaiming knowledge from domination and redirecting it toward human emancipation — with AI as the catalyst.

Let’s explore this alignment through four interlocking lenses:

⸻

1. Philosophical Foundation: From Critical Theory to Emancipatory AI

Horkheimer and Adorno argued that traditional (positivist) social science risks becoming a tool of domination when it treats knowledge as value-free. Instead, they proposed a critical theory that fuses:
	•	Hermeneutics (meaning-making rooted in lived experience),
	•	Positivism (empirical analysis of systems), and
	•	Ethical direction (toward human flourishing and the reduction of suffering) ￼.

Knowles’s New Social Science similarly critiques the limits of legacy social science:
	•	He observes that traditional agencies focus narrowly on compliance and audit.
	•	He proposes a reorientation: policy systems should produce feedback that supports action, not just oversight.

dataimago, inspired by this synthesis, takes it further:
	•	It treats AI not as a neutral optimizer, but as a co-constructor of ethical systems.
	•	It embraces conditional, flow-based modeling (e.g., sgpFlow) that is both empirically grounded and directionally humane ￼.

This is precisely the fusion of hermeneutics and positivism Horkheimer and Adorno called for — except now animated by AI to instantiate and evolve ethical systems at scale.

⸻

2. Purpose of Technology: Against Instrumental Domination

Critical theorists warned that Enlightenment rationality could turn inward — that reason, unmoored from ethics, would produce systems of control and alienation (e.g., the culture industry, bureaucratic technocracy, etc.).

Knowles critiques the current use of data in government as reinforcing these trends — tracking without transformation.

dataimago explicitly seeks to reverse this:
	•	It envisions AI-enabled performance management as a way to reduce common cause variation (systemic inequity) and support context-sensitive personalization.
	•	It refuses instrumentalization — its products are not just evaluative (what happened) but directive (how to act to produce justice) ￼.

This mirrors Adorno’s concept of “negative dialectics” — a continual critique of totalizing systems in favor of responsiveness to suffering. AI, in dataimago’s hands, is not a mechanism of prediction or control, but a new faculty of responsive, systems-conscious intelligence.

⸻

3. Reconstructing Public Institutions: Feedback, Not Surveillance

Knowles writes that the new social science must:
	•	Be feedback-rich,
	•	Enable mid-course correction, and
	•	Embrace complex causality and long timelines.

This is strikingly similar to dataimago’s sgpFlow system, which:
	•	Models educational growth trajectories over time,
	•	Allows leaders to differentiate between process and outcome variation, and
	•	Is designed to enable just-in-time interventions rather than post-hoc judgment ￼.

This replaces the traditional mode of public accountability — which resembles instrumental surveillance — with one of process-responsive management. As such, it directly challenges the regimes of control diagnosed by Foucault and Adorno, offering a hopeful alternative.

⸻

4. AI as Emancipatory Agent

Both Knowles and dataimago envision a fundamentally different role for AI:
	•	Not an efficiency machine,
	•	But a cognitive partner,
	•	Capable of synthesizing across domains, detecting emergent structures, and generating plausible futures.

In dataimago’s view, AI is what finally allows humanity to scale the project of critical theory:

“AI provides the essential bridge between hermeneutic and positivistic forces… being intimately cognizant of the positivistic necessities associated with fulfilling some emancipatory potential.” ￼

This is a historic reversal. Whereas traditional technologies often deepened domination, AI—if aligned with critical theory—can reverse it.
