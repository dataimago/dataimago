Yes, there is a notable — if somewhat indirect — connection between **Alex Karp**, the CEO of Palantir Technologies, and the **Frankfurt School**, particularly through his academic background and intellectual influences.

### 1. **Karp’s Education and Intellectual Formation**

Alex Karp earned a **doctorate in philosophy** (PhD) from **Goethe University in Frankfurt**, where he studied under **Jürgen Habermas**, one of the most prominent second-generation thinkers of the Frankfurt School. This is perhaps the most direct and concrete connection:

* **Habermas** is known for his work on communicative rationality, the public sphere, and his efforts to synthesize Marxist and Kantian traditions in ways that advance human freedom and democratic discourse.
* Karp’s dissertation reportedly focused on **aggression and culture**, which overlaps with themes of **social control**, **rationalization**, and **power** that are central to Frankfurt School theorists like **Adorno**, **Horkheimer**, and **Marcuse**.

So while Karp is not considered a Frankfurt School theorist, he was intellectually shaped by its legacy.

### 2. **Philosophy Meets Surveillance Capitalism?**

The contrast between Karp’s **critical theory education** and his leadership at **Palantir**, a company deeply embedded in government and military intelligence work, has often raised eyebrows. Critical theorists traditionally **criticized the cooptation of reason and technology in service of domination**, especially under capitalism — and Palantir’s operations arguably sit at the center of that complex.

Karp has commented on this tension in interviews:

* He has said that his education gave him a **deep awareness of power**, especially **how data and narratives are used for control**.
* He’s also suggested that **working within the system to safeguard liberal democratic values** is preferable to letting authoritarian regimes dominate the field of advanced technology.

This idea of working with institutions while remaining critical of them — or trying to nudge them toward ethical outcomes — could be seen as a **Habermasian** move, though not without controversy.
